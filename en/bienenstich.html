Bienenstich (Bee Sting Cake)

A crisp almond crust on top, fluffy yeast dough underneath and a delicate vanilla cream in between – for me, Bienenstich is the king of sheet cakes. It takes a few steps, but every single one is worth it at the first bite.

Preparation40 min Baking time30 min Chilling time45 min rising + 30 min chilling Yield12 pieces DifficultyMedium

Instructions

  1. For the yeast dough, dissolve the yeast in the lukewarm milk with 1 teaspoon of the sugar. Knead with the flour, remaining sugar, butter, egg and salt into a smooth dough and let rise, covered, for 45 minutes.
  2. For the topping, melt the butter, sugar, honey and cream in a saucepan and bring briefly to a boil. Stir in the sliced almonds and let the mixture cool a little.
  3. Roll or spread the dough evenly on a parchment-lined sheet pan of about 12 × 16 inches (30 × 40 cm) or in a greased 10-inch (26 cm) springform pan.
  4. Spread the almond mixture evenly over the dough. Bake in a preheated oven at 350°F (180°C) for 25–30 minutes, until the crust is golden and crisp.
  5. Let the cake cool completely. Then cut it horizontally – best to cut it into pieces first and slice each piece individually, so the crust does not break.
  6. For the filling, cook the pudding with 1⅔ cups (400 ml) milk and 2 tablespoons sugar according to the packet directions and let it cool to room temperature, stirring now and then.
  7. Whip the cream with the stabilizer until stiff and fold it into the pudding.
  8. Spread the cream over the bottom halves, place the tops on and chill the Bienenstich for 30 minutes.

Tip

The almond crust turns out especially crisp if the mixture is not too hot when it goes onto the dough. Always store leftovers in the fridge – with its cream filling, this cake is at its best on the first day.
