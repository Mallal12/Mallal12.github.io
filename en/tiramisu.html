Tiramisu (Classic Tiramisu)

Tiramisu is the dessert that makes guests happy: creamy mascarpone, ladyfingers dipped in coffee and a fine layer of cocoa. Good to know: the cream classically contains raw egg yolk, and the alcohol is a matter of taste – simply leave it out if you prefer.

Instructions

  1. Whisk the egg yolks and sugar in a metal bowl over a hot water bath for 5–7 minutes, until pale and creamy. Remove from the heat and let cool slightly – this gently heats the raw yolk.
  2. Stir the mascarpone until smooth and fold in the yolk cream. Whip the cream until stiff and fold it in carefully.
  3. Pour the cooled coffee into a shallow dish and stir in the amaretto, if using.
  4. Dip the ladyfingers one at a time very briefly – only 1–2 seconds – into the coffee and line the bottom of a baking dish (about 8 × 10 inches / 20 × 25 cm) with them. They must not get soggy.
  5. Spread half of the mascarpone cream over them. Add a second layer of dipped ladyfingers and finish with the remaining cream, smoothing the top.
  6. Cover and chill the tiramisu for at least 4 hours, ideally overnight, so it can set and the flavors can meld.
  7. Dust generously with cocoa powder only just before serving and cut into pieces.

Tip

Dip the ladyfingers really only briefly – they keep soaking in the fridge, and the tiramisu turns mushy otherwise. Because of the egg yolk, use only very fresh eggs and keep the dessert chilled at all times.
