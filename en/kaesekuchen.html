Käsekuchen (Classic German Cheesecake with Quark)

A slice of cheesecake belongs on every good coffee table – creamy, not too sweet and with a hint of lemon. With quark and a simple shortcrust base it succeeds even without much practice. At our house it tastes almost better on the second day – if it survives that long.

Preparation25 min Baking time60 min Chilling time3 hrs chilling Yield12 slices DifficultyEasy

Instructions

  1. For the shortcrust, mix the flour, sugar, baking powder and salt. Add the butter and egg and quickly knead into a smooth dough. Wrap and chill for 30 minutes.
  2. Grease a 10-inch (26 cm) springform pan. Roll out the dough on a lightly floured surface, line the pan with it and form a rim about 1¼ inches (3 cm) high. Prick the base several times with a fork.
  3. Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C).
  4. For the filling, stir the quark, sugar, vanilla sugar and pudding powder until smooth. Beat in the eggs one at a time. Add the milk, cream, oil, lemon zest and lemon juice and stir everything into a smooth, rather liquid mixture.
  5. Pour the filling over the base and bake the cake on the middle rack for about 60 minutes, until the surface is lightly golden and the filling only wobbles very slightly.
  6. Turn off the oven, open the door a crack (prop it with a wooden spoon) and let the cheesecake cool down slowly in the oven – this keeps the surface from cracking.
  7. Then chill the cake in the fridge for at least 3 hours, ideally overnight. Only then remove it from the pan and serve.

Tip

The filling is very liquid before baking – that is exactly right, it sets as it bakes. If you do not like lemon, simply replace the zest and juice with a little more vanilla.
